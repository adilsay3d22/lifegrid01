LifeGrid — Live demo script

One story, start to finish: Southfield General Hospital is running out of O+ blood. A manager appeals to donors and a donor answers from their phone. Then a family requests blood for a patient, the hospital confirms it, and the network covers it from stock. Finally the nightly plan, a new donor, and the audit trail. About 35–45 minutes.

Before the demo (5 minutes)

  1. 0.1Reset the demo data (only needed if it has been used since the last reset). In the Neon console open SQL Editor and run DROP SCHEMA public CASCADE; CREATE SCHEMA public;. Then in a terminal, in lifegrid\backend, run .venv\Scripts\python scripts\seed_remote.py and paste the Neon connection string. This rebuilds the clean demo region for today's date.
  2. 0.2Open https://lifegrid01.vercel.app a few minutes early and click once, so the server and database are awake.
  3. 0.3Every staff account's password is synthetic-demo-pass. On the sign-in page, Demo accounts fills the form for you. Only the administrator needs a 6-digit code: add the setup key LIFEGRIDDEMOSECRETKEYAAAAAAAAAAA (time-based) to Google Authenticator or any authenticator app once.
  4. 0.4Phone app sign-in uses a one-time code. No SMS is sent in the demo: the code appears on screen under Development code; tap it to fill it in.
  5. 0.5One browser holds one sign-in. Use a normal window for staff and a phone (or a private window in phone view, F12 then Ctrl+Shift+M) for the donor and requester, side by side. Sign out is at the bottom of the menu.
  6. 0.6To run the demo again later, repeat step 0.1.

What's in the demo data: 14 sites: two regional blood banks (Riverside and Lakeview) and twelve hospitals. About 1,230 red-cell, platelet and plasma units, 8 weeks of usage history, 400 synthetic donors, and a transfer plan that ran "last night". Southfield General Hospital has only 2 O+ red-cell units while its forecast says it will use 7 in the next 2 days. Everything is synthetic.

PART 1 · The manager's morning

Nabila Karim, blood bank manager
Sign in: nabila.karim@lifegrid.test   no code needed
  1. 1.1Network overview opens. Point out the key figures: Red cells available, Expiring in 2 days, Projected stockouts and Open alerts.
  2. 1.2Sites by risk: every hospital and blood bank on the map, coloured Critical, Watch or Stable. Click Southfield to show its counts and the Open site stock link.
  3. 1.3Available stock by site and group: on the Southfield row the O+ cell shows 2. The small bar under each number shows how much expires within 2 days (red), in 3–7 days (amber) or later. Switch to Platelets at the top right: they last only 5 days, so expiry pressure is much higher.
  4. 1.4The Low stock panel at the top lists O+ · Southfield General Hospital first: 2 on hand · 7 needed in 2 days. This comes from the forecast, before anyone has run out.
  5. 1.5Open Forecasts. Choose Southfield General Hospital, Red cells, O+. Black line: the last 28 days of real use. Dashed line: the next 7 days. Shaded band: the likely range. The right panel names the model picked for this series and compares its error with the simple "same day last week" baseline.
What this shows: the whole region on one screen, and a warning that comes from a forecast, days before the shelf is empty.

PART 2 · An appeal reaches a donor's phone

Nabila (staff window) and the donor (phone)
Phone: +8801000000001   code on screen
  1. 2.1Back on the overview, in Low stock, press Appeal to donors on the O+ Southfield row. The dialog Appeal for O+ at Southfield General Hospital has Units needed filled in (projected shortfall 5) and urgency Urgent. Press Send appeal.
  2. 2.2The message reads Appeal AP-… started. 7 donors invited in the first wave. (7 in the daytime; fewer at night, when some donors' quiet hours apply.) The row now says Appeal running. Open Requests: the appeal is listed as Donor appeal. Open it to show the 7 invited donors in wave 1, by reference and blood group only, without names or phone numbers.
  3. 2.3On the phone, open lifegrid01.vercel.app. Tap I want to donate, enter +8801000000001, Send code, tap the development code, Continue. Home shows You can donate and a red bar: 1 open invitation.
  4. 2.4Tap it. The card shows O+, Hospital in Southfield, Urgent, when it is needed, and a Stock appeal label. The donor sees the area, not the hospital's name. Press I can donate. The card changes to Accepted.
  5. 2.5Press Open messages. Type "I can come at 4 pm today." and press Send message. Point out Phone numbers: Not shared.
  6. 2.6Staff window: refresh the appeal. The donor is now Accepted. Press Message next to them and reply "Thank you! Please come to the blood bank desk, ground floor." The reply appears on the phone.
  7. 2.7Optional: press Share my number on both sides. Numbers appear only once both have agreed. Then sign out of the staff window.
What this shows: LifeGrid invited only donors who are compatible, eligible, available, nearby and outside their quiet hours, and only a few at a time: enough to expect the shortfall plus 50% to accept. Donor and hospital talk without exposing anyone's number.

The requester (phone), then Tanvir
Phone: +8801000009999   code on screen

A link appears: Review the proposed transfers for this request.

What this shows: a hospital confirms every request, and LifeGrid looks at stock across the whole network before it asks a single donor. Compare Part 2, where stock was the problem and donors were the answer.

PART 5 · Moving units across the network

Nabila approves and dispatches; Tanvir receives and issues
Sign in: nabila.karim@…, then tanvir.rahman@…
  1. 5.1Nabila: Requests → the request RQ-… → Review the proposed transfers for this request. One line: Westwood District Hospital to Southfield General Hospital, B−, 1 unit, For a blood request. Press Approve and confirm Approve transfer of 1 unit?
  2. 5.2Transfers → To dispatch → Dispatch. LifeGrid picks the actual unit, first-expired-first-out, from units with enough shelf life left to travel (5 days for red cells). Press Dispatch. It moves to In transit: Awaiting receipt at destination.
  3. 5.3Still Nabila: open Transfer plan (last night's plan). Projected effect compares expired and short units without and with the plan. Find Lakeview Regional Blood Bank to Southfield General Hospital, O+, 13 units, Projected shortage at destination: the network's answer to the same O+ shortage. Lower it to 10 and press Approve. The dialog notes You reduced this transfer from 13 to 10 units. Optional: Reject a Relay or swap between sites line, to show nothing moves without a person. Sign out.
  4. 5.4Tanvir: Transfers → In transit → Receive on the unit from Westwood, and confirm. The unit is now available at Southfield, ready for the patient's sixth unit.
  5. 5.5Stock: filter status Reserved. The 5 units held for the request are listed. Tick them, press Issue in the bar at the bottom, and confirm Issue 5 units? Click one unit code: its history shows every step with who did it, when and why. Sign out.
What this shows: the optimizer weighs waste, shortages and distance across the region, and a person approves every move. Units travel first-expired-first-out, and a unit's location changes only when the destination receives it.

PART 6 · A new donor joins

A new person, on the phone
Phone: +8801000005555   code on screen
  1. 6.1Tap I want to donate, enter +8801000005555, tap the code, Continue. Because this number is new, Set up your donor profile opens.
  2. 6.2Pick blood group A+. Tap the map near Southfield: the pin is rounded to about 1 km. Birth year 1998, tick I weigh at least 50 kg, read and tick the consent, then press Save and continue. Home says You can donate.
  3. 6.3Set Quiet hours from 22:00 to 07:00 and tick Still contact me during quiet hours for emergencies. Set Availability to Travelling to pause invitations, then back to Available. Changes save as you make them.
  4. 6.4Open My data: what LifeGrid holds (no name, no address, no medical history), Download my data and Delete my data. Don't delete during the demo.
What this shows: privacy by design. Phone numbers are encrypted, the area is rounded, and the donor can see, export or erase their data at any time.

PART 8 · Rules and the audit trail

Sadia Haque (administrator), then Mahmud Alam (auditor)
Sign in: sadia.haque@…   authenticator code: yes
  1. 8.1Sadia: password, then the 6-digit code from the authenticator app, then Verify and continue. Open Administration → Settings.
  2. 8.2Change max_invites_per_30d from 2 to 3 and press Save. Change history on the right shows the old value, the new value, who changed it and when. Rules like shelf life, waiting periods, wave timing and search distances all live here, not in the code.
  3. 8.3Compatibility tab: rows are patients, columns are donor groups, numbers are preference. O− comes last for everyone who isn't O−, so it's kept for the patients who need it. Users tab: role, site scope, authenticator and the Active switch. Sign out.
  4. 8.4Mahmud (mahmud.alam@lifegrid.test, no code): Audit log lists every action from today: the appeal, the request and its confirmation, the transfers, the issued units, the settings change, every sign-in. Press Verify chain: Chain intact. N events verified.
What this shows: every sensitive action is chained to the one before it by a cryptographic hash. If anyone edited a past record, Verify chain names the first event that no longer matches. Auditors can check everything and change nothing.
