LifeGrid

User Manual

How to use LifeGrid to share blood stock across a regional network, request blood for a patient, and give blood when a hospital nearby needs your group.

Version1.0 · phases 0–6 Date3 October 2026 CoversStaff web app · donor and requester phone app

Every screen in this manual comes from the demo build. All sites, units, donors, requests and phone numbers are synthetic. LifeGrid supports decisions; it does not replace clinical judgment, a licensed blood bank system, or local regulations.

Chapter 1

About LifeGrid

Blood is perishable and type-specific. When every hospital manages it alone, one site throws away expiring units while another runs short. LifeGrid gives the whole region one live view of stock and helps people move it, request it and donate it in time.

What you can do with it

  • See the network. Stock at every hospital and blood bank, by blood group and by how soon it expires.
  • Plan ahead. A 7-day demand forecast for every hospital, and a nightly transfer plan that moves units before they expire or before a hospital runs short. A manager approves every move.
  • Request blood. A family member or a hospital asks for blood for a patient. The hospital confirms, LifeGrid checks stock across the network first, and only asks donors for what stock can’t cover.
  • Ask donors when stock runs low. Staff send an appeal; eligible donors nearby get a text message and an invitation in the phone app.
  • Donate. Donors register once, set when they can be contacted, accept invitations and message the hospital, without giving away their phone number.

Two apps

AppWho uses itWhere
Staff web appBlood bank managers, hospital transfusion leads, donor coordinators, administrators, auditorsA computer browser, at /staff. Works on tablets and phones too.

Roles

What you see depends on your role. Staff are linked to one or more sites; LifeGrid checks your role and sites on every action, so menus only show what you can use.

Blood bank managerSees the whole region. Approves transfers, dispatches them, starts donor appeals, manages blood bank stock.
Hospital transfusion leadManages stock at their own hospital, confirms blood requests, receives transfers, records donor outcomes.
Donor coordinatorLooks after donors: deferrals, group verification, appeals, outcomes. Never sees donor contact details.
AdministratorManages users, sites, settings and compatibility rules. Signs in with an authenticator code.
AuditorRead-only access to everything, including the tamper-evident audit log.
Donor · RequesterUse the phone app. Sign in with a one-time code sent to their phone.
Privacy by designLifeGrid stores only what its rules need. Donors have no name in the system, their area is rounded to about 1 km, and no medical detail is kept. Phone numbers are encrypted and shown to the other person in a conversation only when both choose to share them.
Safety boundariesLifeGrid only recommends. A person approves every transfer, a hospital confirms every request, and donations happen only at licensed facilities. There are no payments to donors.
Chapter 2

Getting started

Open the LifeGrid address your organisation gives you. The main address opens the phone app; staff use the link at the bottom of it, or go straight to /staff.

Signing in on the phone app

  1. Tap I want to donate or I need blood for a patient.
  2. Enter your mobile number with the country code, for example +880 17…, and tap Send code.
  3. Type the 6-digit code from the text message and tap Continue. The first time, this creates your account; there is no password to remember.
Codes expireA code is valid for 5 minutes. After 5 wrong attempts the number is locked for 15 minutes, and you can ask for at most 3 codes an hour.

Install the app. If your phone supports it, an Install the app button appears under the two choices. It adds LifeGrid to your home screen so it opens like any other app. You can also use your browser’s “Add to Home screen”.

Signing in as staff

  1. Enter your work email and password (at least 12 characters) and select Sign in.
  2. If your account uses an authenticator app (always the case for administrators), enter the 6-digit code it shows and select Verify and continue.
  3. LifeGrid opens the screen that fits your role: managers see the network overview, hospital leads their site’s stock, coordinators the donor list, auditors the audit log.
Staying signed inYou stay signed in for up to 7 days of use; LifeGrid renews your session in the background. Select Sign out at the bottom of the menu to end it. If an administrator disables your account, you are signed out at once. One browser holds one signed-in person; to try two roles at the same time, use a second browser or a private window.

Staff app on a small screen

On a narrow screen the side menu folds away. Tap the menu button at the top right to open it; tap outside it or press Esc to close it. Tables that are wider than the screen scroll sideways.

Chapter 3

For donors

Register once, keep your availability up to date, and LifeGrid will invite you only when a hospital near you needs your blood group and you are able to donate.

Registering

The first time you sign in as a donor, LifeGrid asks for a short profile.

  1. Your blood group. Tap your group, or I don’t know my group. A coordinator can mark it as verified later from a facility record.
  2. Your area. Tap the map near where you live or work. LifeGrid rounds it to about 1 km before saving it; your exact address is never stored.
  3. Birth year. Used only for the age rule (18–60 by default).
  4. Weight. Tick the box if you weigh at least 50 kg.
  5. Consent. Read the consent text and tick I agree to these terms. You can’t register without it.
  6. Tap Save and continue.
What hospitals seeUntil you accept an invitation, hospitals see only your blood group and area. Coordinators see a reference like D-4F2A9C, never your phone number.

Your home screen

Donation status tells you whether you can donate now. After a donation you have to wait 120 days (the default); after a deferral, until the date a coordinator set. While you wait, the card shows the date you become eligible again, and LifeGrid sends you one reminder on that day.

Open invitations appear as a red bar. Tap it to see them.

Availability pauses invitations without deleting anything:

  • Available: you can be invited.
  • Unavailable or Travelling: no invitations.

Quiet hours stop invitations during the hours you choose, for example 22:00 to 07:00. Tick Still contact me during quiet hours for emergencies if you want emergency requests to reach you anyway.

Invitations per month caps how often LifeGrid asks you (2 by default).

Changes save as soon as you make them.

Answering an invitation

  1. You get a text message: “LifeGrid: urgent need for O+ blood near Southfield…”. Open the app and tap Invitations.
  2. Tap I can donate if you can go, or Decline if not. Declining is fine and does not count against you.
  3. If you accept, the hospital is told and a private conversation opens.
Why you were invitedLifeGrid invites donors whose group is compatible, who are available, past their waiting period, not deferred, within the search distance and outside their quiet hours. It invites a few people at a time, in waves, and stops as soon as enough have accepted, so nobody is asked more than needed.

Messages and phone numbers

Use the conversation to agree on a time and place. Messages stay inside LifeGrid.

Phone numbers stay hidden. If you want to talk by phone, tap Share my number. Numbers appear for both of you only after both sides choose to share.

Donations happen only at the hospital or blood bank. Never hand over blood privately, and never accept payment.

Your data

  • Download my data saves a file with your profile and invitation history.
  • Delete my data removes your group, area, birth year, dates and phone number, and signs you out. Only anonymous counts remain, so the hospital’s statistics stay correct. This can’t be undone.
Chapter 4

Requesting blood for a patient

Use this when a patient you are helping needs blood. The hospital confirms every request before anything happens, then LifeGrid looks for stock first and asks donors only for what is missing.

Making a request

  1. Sign in with I need blood for a patient. Your list of requests opens; tap New request.
  2. Patient blood group: tap the group on the patient’s record.
  3. Component: red cells unless the doctor says platelets or plasma.
  4. Units needed: 1 to 20.
  5. Hospital: where the patient is treated.
  6. Urgency: emergency, urgent or routine. Emergency invites donors faster and over a smaller distance first.
  7. Needed by: the date and time the blood is needed.
  8. Tap Submit request.
LimitsYou can have up to 3 open requests and create up to 5 a day. The hospital and needed-by time are required.

Following your request

The status page shows units secured, how many came from stock, how many donors were notified and how many accepted, plus a four-step progress bar. It refreshes by itself.

StatusWhat it means
Awaiting hospitalThe hospital still has to confirm the request.
ConfirmedConfirmed; stock is being checked.
Covered by stockUnits in the network cover the whole request. No donors were contacted.
Finding donorsStock didn’t cover everything, so eligible donors nearby are being invited for the rest.
FulfilledAll units were secured.
Partly fulfilledThe needed-by time passed with some units secured.
UnfilledThe needed-by time passed with no units secured.
CancelledYou or the hospital withdrew the request. Reserved units are released.

When donors accept, a Donors who accepted list appears on the status page. Tap a donor to message them; phone numbers are shared only if you both choose to share.

My requests lists everything you have asked for, newest first. Tap a request to open its status page.

To withdraw a request, open it, tap Cancel request and confirm. Any units reserved for it go back to stock and donor invitations stop.

Chapter 5

For blood bank managers

You see the whole region. Your daily work: check stock and low-stock warnings, review the overnight transfer plan, approve and dispatch transfers, and appeal to donors when a group runs low.

Network overview

  • Low stock lists red-cell groups where a hospital holds fewer units than it is forecast to use in the next 2 days, worst first. Each row has Appeal to donors (see Appealing to donors).
  • Key figures: units available, units expiring within 2 days, projected stockouts and open alerts.
  • Sites by risk: every hospital and blood bank on the map, coloured critical, watch or stable. Click a site for its counts and a link to its stock.
  • Available stock by site and group: one row per site, one column per blood group. The small bar under each number shows how much expires within 2 days (red), in 3–7 days (amber) or later (grey). Red numbers mean none on hand. Switch between red cells, platelets and plasma at the top right.
  • Run plan now recalculates the transfer plan immediately; Review tonight’s plan opens it.

Appealing to donors when stock runs low

  1. In Low stock, select Appeal to donors on the group and hospital you want to cover.
  2. Check Units needed (the projected shortfall is filled in) and choose the urgency.
  3. Select Send appeal. LifeGrid invites eligible donors near that hospital in waves, by text message and in the app, until enough accept. Donors see the group, the area and the urgency only.
  4. The row now shows Appeal running; you can’t start a second appeal for the same group and hospital while one is open.
How waves workEach wave invites just enough donors to expect the shortfall plus 50% to accept. If not enough accept within the wait (10 minutes for emergency, 30 for urgent, 2 hours for routine), the next wave searches a wider area. If nobody accepts after the last wave, coordinators and bank managers are notified.

Follow an appeal on the Requests screen: appeals are marked Donor appeal. Open one to see which donors were invited and who accepted, and select Message next to a donor who accepted to talk to them.

Site stock

  • Choose the site with the drop-down at the top right (managers can open any site).
  • Filter by status, component, group and expiry band. Filters are kept in the address, so you can bookmark or share a filtered view.
  • Remaining shows days left, or hours on the last day, in red when 2 days or fewer remain.
  • Select a unit code to open its history.

Changing units

  1. Tick the units. The action bar appears at the bottom.
  2. Choose an action. Every action shows the unit count and the codes before anything changes.
  3. For Quarantine and Discard you must give a reason.
ActionFromWho
IssueReserved (for a confirmed request)Hospital lead
QuarantineAvailable, reserved, in transitHospital lead, bank manager
Clear quarantineQuarantinedBank manager
DiscardQuarantined (bank manager) or expiredHospital lead, bank manager

Units expire automatically at their expiry time; reservations not issued within 24 hours return to stock.

Receiving and importing units

Receive unit records one released, labelled unit arriving at the site: unit code, group, component, collection and expiry. Import CSV takes a file (or pasted text) with the columns unit_code, abo, rh, component, collected_at, expires_at; select Download template for an example.

Unit history

Forecasts

Choose a site, a component and a group. The chart shows the last 28 days of actual use (black), the forecast for the next 7 days (dashed red) and the likely range (shaded, 10th to 90th percentile). The panel on the right names the model chosen for this series, its error compared with the simple “same day last week” baseline, and how often actual use fell inside the range. Forecasts are recalculated every night at 01:30.

Transfer plan

Every night at 02:00 LifeGrid works out which transfers would cut both waste and shortages over the next 3 days, at low transport cost. Projected effect compares expired and short units with and without the plan.

Each recommendation shows the route, group, component, distance, reason and expected benefit:

  • Expiry risk at source: the units would expire unused where they are.
  • Projected shortage at destination: the destination is forecast to run short.
  • Relay or swap between sites: part of a chain that helps a third site.
  • For a blood request: proposed when a hospital confirmed a request and stock was elsewhere.
  1. Lower the unit count if you want to move fewer units.
  2. Select Approve (confirm in the dialog) or Reject. Nothing moves without approval.
  3. Approved lines appear under Transfers, ready to dispatch.

Transfers

  1. Select Dispatch. LifeGrid picks the actual units first-expired-first-out, only from units with enough shelf life left to travel (5 days for red cells by default).
  2. Check the list and select Dispatch. The units become In transit; their location changes only when the destination receives them.

Requests

Managers see every request and appeal. Select one to see its progress, its donors and, for requests covered from other sites, a link to review the proposed transfers.

Chapter 6

For hospital transfusion leads

You look after stock at your hospital, confirm requests for your patients, receive transfers and record whether donors donated.

Your hospital’s stock

Stock actions work as described in Site stock. You can issue reserved units to patients, quarantine and discard.

Confirming a request

  1. Open the request under needs confirmation and check it against the patient’s record.
  2. Select Confirm request. LifeGrid then:
    • reserves compatible units at your hospital, best match first, then first-expired-first-out;
    • proposes transfers from other sites for anything still missing (a bank manager approves them);
    • invites donors only for the units stock can’t cover (red cells). Platelet and plasma shortfalls go to the coordinators instead.
  3. The requester sees each step on their phone. Use Cancel request if it is no longer needed; reserved units go back to stock.
Your own requestsRequests you create with New request on this screen are confirmed for your hospital straight away.

Recording donor outcomes

When a donor who accepted comes in (or doesn’t), open the request, find the donor in the list and select Donated, No-show or Deferred on site. A donation counts towards units secured and resets that donor’s waiting period. Outcomes are the only thing that changes a donor’s reliability score. The screenshot in chapter 7 shows the buttons.

Receiving transfers

On Transfers, the In transit tab lists transfers coming to your hospital. When the box arrives, select Receive and confirm the unit codes. The units become available at your hospital.

Chapter 7

For donor coordinators

You keep the donor list accurate and safe: deferrals, verified groups, appeals and outcomes. You never see donors’ names or contact details.

Donor list

  • Search by donor reference, filter by group and eligibility.
  • A solid group chip with a check mark means the group is verified; a dashed chip means self-reported.
  • Eligibility shows Eligible now, a deferral date, or the date the waiting period ends.
  • Reliability is built from past responses and donations (0 to 1).

Deferrals and group verification

Select Defer, pick a category and the date the donor can donate again. Only the category and date are stored, never medical detail. The donor is not invited until that date.

Select Verify group when you have confirmed a donor’s group from a facility record. Verified donors are preferred when scores are tied.

Appeals

Coordinators can start donor appeals from the same Low stock panel as bank managers; see Appealing to donors.

Chapter 8

For administrators and auditors

Administrators manage who can do what, and the rules LifeGrid follows. Every change is versioned and written to a tamper-evident audit log that auditors can check at any time.

Users

  • Change a user’s role with the drop-down. Role and scope changes are audited.
  • Turn Active off to disable an account; that person is signed out everywhere at once.
  • Administrators must use an authenticator; the column shows Required until one is set up.

Sites

Settings

Business rules live here rather than in the software: shelf lives, minimum shelf life for transfers, expiry alert windows, waiting period after donation, age limits, invitation limits, wave timing and search distances, plan time and solver limits, request limits. Edit a value and select Save. Values with several parts (for example per component) are edited as text in the form {"red_cells": 5, "platelets": 1, "plasma": 30}. The history records the old and new value, who changed it and when.

Clinical valuesShelf life, compatibility, eligibility and waiting periods are prototype defaults. A qualified clinician must review them against local standards before real use.

Compatibility rules

LifeGrid never suggests a unit or donor outside this table. Exact matches always come first, and O− red cells come last for anyone who isn’t O−, so they are kept for those who need them. Switch between red cells, platelets and plasma at the top.

Audit log

Every sensitive action is recorded: sign-ins and failed sign-ins, role changes, unit changes, transfer decisions, request confirmations, phone sharing, data export and deletion, settings changes. Each event is chained to the one before by a cryptographic hash. Verify chain recomputes the whole chain; if any stored event had been edited, LifeGrid names the first one that doesn’t match. Auditors can search and verify but change nothing.

ProblemWhat to do

“Too many attempts”The number is locked for 15 minutes after 5 wrong codes.
Signed out unexpectedlySessions end after 7 days, when an administrator disables the account, or if an old session token is reused (a safety measure). Sign in again.

“You do not have access to this site”Your role or site scope doesn’t include it. Ask an administrator.
